3D forced reduced-MHD (spectral z) — WebGPU pseudospectral solver

rfftn over (z,x,y) (numpy layout) · LSRK33 with the exact 2×2 Alfvén propagator · Elsasser Ornstein–Uhlenbeck forcing · fp32 · ← 2D version · overview
z is spectral: the Alfvén coupling ±i·kz is the off-diagonal of the 2×2 linear operator and is propagated EXACTLY, so there is no wave CFL — dt is set by perpendicular advection alone. The display is one z slice of the 3D field (slider above); vectors are the perpendicular components on that slice. The “cube faces” modes instead draw the three visible boundary faces of the box (z = Lz, x = Lx, y = Ly) in an oblique view, like examples/forced-turbulence-3D.ipynb; the slice slider, the vectors and the cut trace are inactive there. “dual view” adds a second canvas with its own quantity AND its own z slice (cube faces, arrows and the cut trace stay on display 1). |z±| are the Elsasser fields z± = φ±ψ (shown as |ž×∇z±|); σ_c = (|z⁺|²−|z⁻|²)/(|z⁺|²+|z⁻|²) is drawn on a FIXED ±1 range and set to 0 wherever the local Elsasser energy is below 10⁻⁴ of its maximum on the displayed slice. dt is recomputed every cfl_every steps (forced to every step for the first 200 steps of a run — a frozen dt from a quiescent start blows up). hyper=1 needs MUCH larger diss to resolve — “auto” picks a resolved diss for the current hyper, resolution and injected power. z_diss_k is a kz4 stabiliser (“auto” = 3/kz,c³), not a power input; the bottom slider position is exactly 0. 256²×64 needs a good GPU (~0.5 GB of buffers); 64²×256 (~0.14 GB) is the long-box option. Lz is a free parameter of the grid — kz, the z_diss_k auto value, the parallel-spectrum abscissa and the packet envelopes all follow it (the cube view still draws the box as a cube, whatever the aspect ratio). The initial condition selector picks what Reset loads: the built-in large-scale modes, an exactly quiescent state, or “letter packets” — one rasterized, gaussian-smoothed glyph per Elsasser field times a gaussian z-envelope (σz = Lz/16), each scaled so that max |ž×∇z±| equals the amplitude knob (approximately: the 2/3 dealias trims it slightly). z+ travels toward SMALLER z and z- toward larger z at vA = 1, so the packets are placed at 11Lz/16 and 5Lz/16 and collide at z = Lz/2; see the collision demo. “custom” opens the blob editor: press edit IC (this pauses the run), then click or drag on the display to deposit periodic 3D gaussian blobs into the chosen field — z± are the Elsasser stream functions, φ/ψ the evolved ones (φ = a blob in both z±, ψ one of each sign). Each blob is centred on the z plane display 1 is currently showing, with a gaussian z-envelope of width σz (both σ sliders are fractions of the box and are floored at 2 cells); the amplitude slider is its peak |ž×∇| and the “negative” box — or dragging with the RIGHT button — flips the sign. The canvas previews the drawn STREAM FUNCTION on that plane, on the CPU (no GPU round trip while editing); apply & run uploads it, clear erases (there is no undo stack), and Reset re-applies the same drawing until you change preset, resolution or Lz. Drawing is unavailable while display 1 is in cube-face mode. Either display’s z slice can track the plane of maximum z+ or z- energy instead of following its slider (per-plane Elsasser energies are reduced on the GPU and read back ~10×/s; the tracked plane and its z coordinate appear in the readout).

Energy trace: one sample per rendered frame (history capped at 2000 points, decimated 2:1 when full). Spectra: perpendicular shell-binned Eu(k⊥), Eb(k⊥) summed over kz (solid) and the parallel spectra E(k∥) summed over k⊥ (dashed), recomputed at most ~3×/s. Cut: the displayed field along y at x = Lx/2 on the current z slice, ~10×/s.
