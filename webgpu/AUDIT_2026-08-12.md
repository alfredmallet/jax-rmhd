Scope: `common.js`, `physics.js`, `rmhd2d.html`, `rmhd3d.html`, `style.css`, `index.html`.
`devtools/` read for context but not audited as shipped code. No GPU was available, so every
efficiency claim below is a static read plus arithmetic, not a measurement.

## Verdict

The shared core is in good shape. Duplication is genuinely low and the places it survives are
small and named. There is no efficiency problem on the numerical side. There *is* one on the
graphical side, and it is concentrated in a single function.

Ranked by expected payoff:

1. Volume raymarch samples a storage buffer by hand — 537 M scalar loads per frame per card.
2. Every display card re-renders every rAF frame whether or not anything changed, paused included.
3. FFT twiddles are recomputed with `cos`/`sin` in the innermost loop — ~2 G transcendentals/step at max resolution.
4. ~64 MB of per-display-card GPU scratch that could be shared or borrowed.
5. ~200 lines of real duplication between the two app scripts.
6. Three pieces of confirmed dead code.

## 1. Duplication

**Within the shared core: none.** `devtools/dup.py` over `common.js` + `physics.js` reports zero
10-line clone groups, and zero clones reaching from an app into the core. The chart primitives are
properly factored — `chartFrame`, `logTicks`, `legend`, `xTick`/`yTick`, `drawTimeSeries` each have
6–8 call sites. `fitLogSlope` has exactly two wrappers rather than two implementations. The
colormap has one source (`CMAP_COEF`), pinned by `cmapcheck.js`. All 42 CSS classes are used.
This is better than most codebases this size, and it is clearly the result of deliberate work
(H.0, ONEPAGE, PINCURVE).

**Between the two app scripts: ~200 lines, in five clusters.** `dup.py` over the extracted
inline scripts finds 17 clone groups. Collapsed:

| what | 2D | 3D | similarity | shareable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `rowsR2C` / `rowsC2R` FFT kernel declarations | ~30 | ~30 | 0.91 / 0.94 | yes — template in `physics.js` |
| `colsFwd` / `colsInv` declarations | ~10 | ~10 | high | yes, same place |
| `makeGrid` perpendicular half | ~45 | ~60 | 0.57 | partly — `makePerpGrid(p)` helper |
| `DISP_FIELDS` table | 20 | 20 | 0.84 | yes — one table, 3D drops nothing |
| `drawNoise` reality symmetrization + `step()` | ~35 | ~35 | high | yes |
| `runSelfTest` scaffolding (`loadA`, row idiom, encode-read-compare) | 121 | 205 | 0.66 | ~60 lines of harness |

The FFT declarations are the cleanest win and fit the existing architecture exactly: `physics.js`
already holds templates over a per-app constants object, and the only differences are grid
constants and the 3D `lpb` batching argument. `DISP_FIELDS` is a verbatim copy — the 3D comment
even explains that the cube is a *view*, orthogonal to the list, which is precisely the argument
for the list being shared.

**HTML markup: ~30 lines of skeleton per page**, most of it comments explaining that the real
content is built in `common.js`. Irreducible in substance; the comments could be one line each.

## 2. Conciseness

`common.js` is 6508 lines, of which **2328 (36% of non-blank) are comment-only**; `physics.js` is
41%, `rmhd3d`'s script 29%. That is not automatically wrong — most of these comments are load-bearing
"why", which is the good kind. But roughly 8% of them (175 lines in `common.js`, 68 in `rmhd3d`)
are *provenance*: `REFINE_PLAN I2.3`, `FEEDBACK_2026-08-10 item 12`, `adversarial review
2026-08-12, MINOR 2`, dated behaviour-change notes. That is changelog living in the hot path.
It duplicates 3631 lines of plan `.md` files and 937 lines of `README.md` that already carry it.
Stripping tags while keeping the reasoning would remove ~250 lines and, more usefully, stop the
comments reading as a project diary. This is a taste call, not a defect.

Two structural size problems:

- **`DisplayCard` is 844 lines and ~50 methods, of which ~470 lines and ~20 methods are
  recording** (`recToggle` … `recBailWC`, plus the WebCodecs pool and the MP4 path). The recording
  machinery is more than half of a class whose job is "show a field". It has its own state
  (`this.wc`, `this.rec`, pools, watchdog, drop counters) and touches the card only through
  `render()`, the canvas, and the footer. Extracting a `Recorder` owned by the card would halve
  the class without changing a byte of behaviour. The `devtools/bootstub.js` legs are already
  written against observable behaviour (blobs, downloads, `caps`), so they would survive it.
- **`drawCut` is 255 lines**, `drawSpectrum` 171, `drawGen2D` 111. `drawCut` is the outlier.

One near-duplicate the token detector cannot see: `drawSpectrum` (1429–1500) and `drawAniso`
(1718–1790) share ~19 lines of identical log-log frame setup — `chartFrame`, two `logTicks`
sweeps, the dashed forcing-shell band, the legend call. A `logLogFrame(c, W, H, P, xmax, ...)`
helper would absorb it. Modest; mentioned for completeness.

## 3. Efficiency — numerical side

Structurally sound and, as you expected, faithful to `taranis`: the self-test gates both apps
against fp64 reference vectors from the repo, through the production pipelines, at
fp32-appropriate tolerances. Buffers are allocated once per solver, pipelines and bind groups
built once, one command encoder and one submit per step, no per-step CPU work beyond drawing
~12 OU noise modes. `reduceTail` is a proper tree reduction. In-place transforms in `gradsK`
avoid the 2D page's ping-pong buffer. The `lpb` batching around
`maxComputeWorkgroupsPerDimension` is correct and documented.

**One real knob: the FFT recomputes twiddles per butterfly.**

```wgsl
let ang: f32 = -PI * f32(k) / f32(p);
let wc: f32 = cos(ang);
let ws: f32 = sin(ang);
```

Butterfly count for one 3D field transform at 256²×64 is 31.6 M (16.8 M in y, 8.5 M in x,
6.3 M in z). LSRK33 does ~30 field transforms per step, so **~1 G butterflies and ~2 G `sin`/`cos`
evaluations per step at max resolution**. Each butterfly is ~10 arithmetic ops plus two
transcendentals; on hardware where a transcendental is 4–8× a multiply, the twiddles are
plausibly 20–40% of FFT time — and the FFT is essentially all of the solver.

A precomputed twiddle table in a read-only storage buffer (`N/2` `vec2<f32>` per axis, ≤ 2 kB,
uploaded once) removes them. Accuracy is *unchanged or better* — a table computed on the CPU in
f64 beats an f32 `PI*k/p` division followed by an f32 `cos` — so this is not the usual
speed-for-precision trade. Precondition: it changes the emitted WGSL, so it must be landed with a
fresh `dumpwgsl2` baseline and the reference vectors re-run; `checkiso`/`check2dspec`'s
byte-identity gates will fire by design.

Note also `var<workgroup> buf: array<vec2<f32>, 2N>` = 16 N bytes, which caps line length at
N = 1024 against the 16 kB minimum workgroup storage limit. Fine today; a ceiling worth knowing.

## 4. Efficiency — graphical side

This is where the headroom is.

### 4.1 The volume raymarch does software trilinear filtering (`rmhd3d.js:855`)

`vsamp` reads **eight scalars from a storage buffer per sample** and interpolates by hand, also
returning the finite-difference gradient from the same eight corners. At 512² pixels ×
`VOL_STEPS = 256`:

- 67 M samples per frame per volume card
- **537 M scalar buffer loads per frame** — 32 G loads/s if the card ever hits 60 fps

There is an early-out at `aa > VOL_AMAX` and no empty-space skipping. This is, by a wide margin,
the most expensive thing the app does, and it is the path a phone hits.

The fix is the obvious one: put the field in a `texture_3d` with a linear sampler. One hardware
fetch replaces eight buffer loads, filtering is free, and the texture cache sees a tiled layout
instead of a linear one. Two honest caveats:

- `r32float` is **not** filterable in WebGPU without the optional `float32-filterable` feature.
  `r16float` is filterable everywhere, so this is an fp16 display path. Purely visual — but
  `checkiso`'s Phase B leg compares the executed fragment shader against a CPU reference march
  at ~1e-6 achieved / 1e-5 tolerance, and that tolerance would have to be re-derived.
- The gradient needs either three extra fetches or an `rgba16float` volume carrying value +
  gradient (33.5 MB at 256²×64, written by the existing prep pass). The latter is one fetch for
  everything and probably the right shape.

Consistency argument in favour: the cube-faces path already renders through textures (`ftex`,
`rmhd3d.js:1408`). The volume is the odd one out.

Cheaper interim measures, in case the texture change is too invasive: render the volume at half
canvas resolution and upscale (4× off the top), or drop `VOL_STEPS` while the solver is running
and restore it when paused. The second is defensible — `VOL_STEPS = 256` was measured as
*converged* for a still picture, which is a stronger requirement than a frame you see for 16 ms.

### 4.2 Display cards render unconditionally, every frame

`common.js:6322`, inside `loop()`:

```js
if (!icDraw.on) for (const d of cards.disp) { d.render(); ... }
```

There is no dirty gate. A **paused** app with a volume card open still runs the full raymarch at
rAF rate forever, and so does a paused app with three slice cards. Nothing has changed: not the
state, not the mode, not the colormap.

A `dirty` flag set by `apply()`, `setDisplayMode`, `_resize`, a step, and forced true while
`this.wc || this.rec` is live, would cost ~10 lines and eliminate the entire idle GPU load. This
is the single best effort-to-payoff item in the audit, and it matters most on exactly the device
the recent recording work was about — a paused phone currently burns battery at full tilt.

Caveat to respect: `recCapture()` must keep seeing a freshly rendered frame in the same
synchronous task (the reason it lives there at all, RECRAF), so the flag must be forced true
during a take. That is a one-line condition, and `bootstub`'s rAF-capture legs would catch a
mistake.

### 4.3 Full pipeline drain plus a map round trip every frame

```js
await device.queue.onSubmittedWorkDone();
...
const s = await solver.readStats();
```

Two sync points per frame. The drain is deliberate — it is what makes `ms` a real GPU time and
drives `stepsPerFrame` — and the readback is one 48-byte map, so the cost is latency, not
bandwidth. But together they mean the GPU idles through the readback, the chart drawing and the
DOM updates every frame. Reading stats one frame late (the pattern already used for arrows, the
colorbar and the cut line, with the `sv === solver` guard) would let the next frame's steps be
submitted before the previous frame's numbers land. Worth doing only if profiling says the gap is
real; unlike 4.1 and 4.2 this one has a genuine correctness-of-timing argument on the other side.

Minor and related: one `submit()` per display card per frame plus one for the step batch. Merging
the card renders into a single encoder would cut submits from 1+N to 2.

### 4.4 `readBuf` allocates and destroys a staging buffer per call (`common.js:384`)

```js
const st = device.createBuffer({ size: byteLen, usage: COPY_DST | MAP_READ });
... await st.mapAsync(READ); ... st.unmap(); st.destroy();
```

Called at least once per frame (`readStats`), plus arrows at ~10 Hz per card, the colorbar at
~3 Hz per card, the cut line at 10 Hz and the spectra at 3.3 Hz. So roughly 1–6 GPU buffer
create/destroy pairs per frame, forever. Buffer creation is not free in any WebGPU implementation
and this is the one allocation hot spot in an otherwise allocation-free frame. A small pool keyed
by size, or a persistent staging buffer per call site, is a contained change — `readBuf` has one
body and every caller `await`s it.

### 4.5 Per-card GPU memory

At 256²×64, computed from the constructors:

| | MB |
|---|---|
| `gradsK` (8 complex volumes) | 129 |
| `realGrads` | 128 |
| `fields`, `delta`, `rhs`, `nlk`, `realNL` | 32 each |
| **solver total** | **~420** |
| per display chain (`dispK`, `dispK2`, `dispR`, `dispR2`) | **~64** |
| 1 card / 3 cards | 484 / 612 |

`gradsK` at 129 MB is a single allocation half of WebGPU's default 256 MB `maxBufferSize`. That
is a real ceiling on any future resolution bump, not just a number.

The per-chain 64 MB is avoidable. Submits execute in order on one queue — the argument the code
*already* makes for `readFieldLines` and the gen2d sweep borrowing `gradsK`/`realGrads` — so the
display chains' k-space and real scratch could be borrowed from the same RHS scratch, or shared
as one set across all cards. That takes 3 cards from 612 MB to ~484 MB and makes opening a card
nearly free. Risk is real and should be stated: it couples cards to each other and to the step,
and any future asynchronous consumer of `dispR` between submits would break silently. The
per-card `arrows` and `maxVal` buffers must stay per-card (they are read asynchronously) — they
are kilobytes, so that is no loss.

## 5. Dead code

- **`clearStatus` (`common.js:53`)** — zero references anywhere in the shipped set. Delete.
- **`gen2dRidge` (`common.js:1982`)** — referenced only by `devtools/check2dspec.js`. The app
  ships a function no page calls, and a test asserts against something no visitor sees. Either the
  ridge belongs on the plot or the function belongs in the checker.
- **The unbanded `parFL` pass in `gen2dSpec` (`rmhd3d.js:2445`)** — already self-documented as
  orphaned since the 2026-08-11 feedback round, kept deliberately because removing it changes the
  sweep's `total` and the checks pinning it. It costs one extra 256-line field-line march plus a
  full gradient pass per generate press, i.e. ~1/(nbands+1) of a ~1 s one-shot. Low urgency, but
  it is now the oldest known-dead thing in the tree and the comment has started to acquire
  sub-comments. Worth closing.

## 6. A note on the cost of acting on any of this

`devtools/` is ~10 k lines against ~11.6 k lines of app, and several gates pin *emitted WGSL byte
for byte* against a base commit, plus canvas call logs and Mode uniform bytes. That is excellent
for confidence and it means every suggestion above has a defined verification path — but it also
means "tidy up the FFT declarations" is not a five-minute change. Sequence accordingly: the two
items that touch no WGSL and no drawn pixels (4.2 dirty-render, 4.4 staging pool, and the dead
code) are cheap and independent. 4.1 and the twiddle table each need a fresh baseline and a
tolerance re-derivation. The app-script deduplication (§1) is the one place where the WGSL must
come out byte-identical, which `dumpwgsl2` + `kdiff.py` will tell you outright.

## Postscript, 2026-08-12 — §4.3 was underrated, 6 and 7 were overrated

An on-device reading (iPhone 11, 2D 256² forced, recording) settled which of the remaining
items matters: **`gap` 68 ms, 40 steps/s, `drop` rising steadily.**

- The loop pass is ~68 ms and `stepsPerFrame` ≈ 2.7, so passes run at ~15/s. Since RECRAF
  the loop is the recorder's feeder — one capture per pass, never backfilled — so the
  encoder is **starved**, not slow, and `drop` is a loop measurement wearing a recorder's
  name.
- The controller holds `ms ≤ 22`, and `ms` stops before every readback: **≥ 46 ms of each
  pass is latency it cannot see.** Not GPU compute — completion messages returning to JS on
  Safari's task granularity, with the GPU idle across them because nothing is submitted
  until they arrive.
- `ms` also *contains* the drain's latency, so the ~8 ms/step it implies is an over-estimate;
  the step is probably 1–2 ms against ~0.3 ms of FFT arithmetic.

So the ranking in §"Suggested order" below is wrong for this device, and the corrected one is:

1. **§4.3 — `LOOPLAT_PLAN.md`. DONE 2026-08-12** (§1–§3; §4, the display-render cap, left
   for a measurement no sandbox can make). ~46 ms of a 68 ms pass. Expect 3–12× on
   `steps/s` and `drop` → 0. No WGSL changes — and both pages' dumps came out byte-identical,
   which for a pure-control-flow change is the assertion, not a formality. The on-device
   reading is still owed; `?recdebug` grew a `loop: pass/sf/ifl` line to make it.
2. **Dispatch count inside the step** (~29 calls in 3 compute passes). Not planned yet.
3. **Item 7 — `VOLTEX_PLAN.md`.** Real, but it is the 3D volume view only and has nothing to
   do with this symptom. Its Phase D (half-res, fewer `VOL_STEPS` while moving) is the cheap
   part.
4. **Item 6 — the twiddle table.** Optimises ~0.3 ms of a 68 ms pass. **Close it** unless a
   profile after (1) and (2) says the FFT became the bottleneck.
